\chapter{Observability, Evaluation and Testing}
\label{ch:observability}

\begin{quote}\itshape
Without an evaluation set you are not changing a prompt. You are guessing and
shipping.
\end{quote}

\noindent
Three subjects, one argument: you cannot improve what you cannot see, and you
cannot change what you cannot re-measure. This is the chapter that separates
people who have run an agent in production from people who have built one.
It covers tracing, the evaluation discipline that makes prompt changes safe, and
a testing strategy that puts a real suite in CI without a single billed model
call.

\chapterstub{
\textbf{Argument.} Non-determinism does not make agents untestable; it makes
\emph{text assertions} untestable. Assert on behaviour --- which tool was called,
whether the schema validates, how many steps it took --- and the suite is as
deterministic as any other.

\textbf{Sections.} (14.1) Tracing with LangSmith: environment-variable
activation, what a trace contains, \api{@traceable} for your own functions.
(14.2) The alternatives when a client will not accept a SaaS backend:
OpenTelemetry, Langfuse, Phoenix --- with what you give up. (14.3) The metrics
worth a dashboard: latency, time to first token, cost per conversation, step
count, task completion. (14.4) Evaluation: datasets, evaluators, and the split
between deterministic checks and judged ones. (14.5) Trajectory evaluation ---
scoring the path, not just the answer, which is the agent-specific part.
(14.6) LLM-as-judge: construction, calibration against human labels, and the
failure modes that make an uncalibrated judge worse than no judge. (14.7)
Evaluating RAG: retrieval scored separately from generation, and why combining
them hides the actual defect. (14.8) Evaluation in CI: regression thresholds,
prompt A/B tests, and handling flakiness without disabling the gate. (14.9)
Testing: fake models, node tests, the integration layer kept small and marked.
(14.10) Prompt versioning and changing a prompt without a deploy.

\textbf{Listings.} A fake chat model returning scripted tool calls; a node test;
a twenty-case evaluation set with three evaluator types; the CI job with a
regression threshold; a judge calibrated against hand labels, with its agreement
rate reported.

\textbf{Diagrams.} \code{obs-trace-anatomy} --- one agent run as a trace tree
with cost and latency annotated. \code{eval-ladder} --- deterministic checks,
judged checks, human review, by cost and by confidence. \code{test-pyramid-agents}
--- what is tested at which layer, and what a real model call is reserved for.

\textbf{Mini-project.} Stage 11: tracing wired up, a twenty-case evaluation set
running in CI with a threshold, and a full test suite that makes no real model
calls.
