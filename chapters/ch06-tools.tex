\chapter{Tools and Structured Output}
\label{ch:tools}

\begin{quote}\itshape
The docstring is not documentation. It is part of the prompt.
\end{quote}

\noindent
A tool is a function the model can decide to call, and the thing engineers
consistently underestimate is that the function's name, its type hints, its field
descriptions and its docstring are all sent to the model. They are not
commentary --- they are the specification the model routes on. A tool that works
perfectly and is described vaguely will be called at the wrong times, and nothing
in your test suite will tell you.

\chapterstub{
\textbf{Argument.} Tool selection quality is an engineering property with a
measurable degradation curve. It falls off somewhere around fifteen to twenty
tools, and there are three structural answers --- a selector, subagents, or MCP
namespacing --- rather than a prompt-wording answer.

\textbf{Sections.} (6.1) \api{@tool}: the decorator, the inferred schema, and what
the model actually receives. (6.2) Explicit schemas with \code{args\_schema} and
Pydantic \api{Field} descriptions. (6.3) Async tools --- and what LangChain does
with a sync-only tool under \api{ainvoke} (a thread-pool hop, which works and
costs). (6.4) Tool errors: how a failure becomes a \api{ToolMessage} the model can
recover from, and why the default is a configuration decision rather than a given.
(6.5) The tool-calling mechanics: the round trip, parallel tool calls, and what
the message list looks like at each step. (6.6) Scaling past twenty tools: the
selector middleware, subagent partitioning, MCP. (6.7) Structured output:
\api{response\_format}, and the three strategies --- provider-native, tool-based,
automatic --- with the cases each is right for. (6.8) Why prompted JSON is a
pre-1.0 pattern and is not coming back.

\textbf{Listings.} Five async tools for the mini-project (search, SQL, HTTP,
metrics, send-email); the same tool with a poor and a good description, with a
selection-accuracy measurement between them; error handling that returns a usable
message rather than a stack trace; structured output into a Pydantic model.

\textbf{Diagrams.} \code{tool-round-trip} --- sequence diagram of a two-tool turn
including a parallel call. \code{tool-schema-to-prompt} --- which parts of a
Python function end up in the model's context.

\textbf{Measurement.} Tool-selection accuracy over a fixed set of thirty queries,
with five tools then twenty, and with terse then descriptive docstrings. Four
cells, twenty runs each. This is the chapter's original data and it is cheap.

\textbf{Mini-project.} Stage 03: the five tools, async, with schemas, tests and
a fake-model harness.
