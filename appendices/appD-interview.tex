\chapter{Interview Preparation}
\label{app:interview}

\noindent
This book began as preparation material for a specific conversation, and this
appendix is what remains of that. It is also, less obviously, a decent
self-assessment: if you can answer these without reaching for the chapter, you
have the material.

\chapterstub{
\textbf{Contents.} (D.1) The three-week plan, day by day, with a practical task
per day --- for a reader who needs a schedule rather than a book. (D.2) Async
Python questions with model answers. (D.3) LangChain questions. (D.4) LangGraph
questions. (D.5) Production and system-design questions, including the
``design a service for a thousand concurrent users'' exercise worked in full.
(D.6) Context engineering and cost questions --- a gap in the source material and
increasingly the part that distinguishes candidates. (D.7) Typical live-coding
tasks: write a tool, write a node, debug a runaway loop --- with what an assessor
is actually watching for. (D.8) Typical take-home briefs and how they are marked.
(D.9) Questions worth asking the interviewer, and what each answer tells you
about the team. (D.10) Phrasings to avoid, paired with what to say instead.
(D.11) Framing a move from another ecosystem: what a decade of backend
engineering is genuinely worth here, stated without either apology or
overstatement.

\textbf{Note.} The answers here are compressed. Each cross-references the chapter
that argues the case properly, because an answer you can only recite does not
survive a follow-up question.
}
