Every listing was executed against the pinned versions, so nothing carries a run this before you trust it marker. What remains is finishing: three measurements that need a provider budget, the mini-project's stages, and one screenshot — each counted on every CI build.

Why

LangChain 1.0 was published on 17 October 2025. It removed the three abstractions almost every earlier tutorial was built on — AgentExecutor, initialize_agent and LLMChain — and inverted the relationship between the two libraries: langchain is now a thin layer over langgraph's runtime, and create_agent returns a compiled LangGraph graph.

The consequence is unusually severe. Most LangChain material on the internet describes an architecture that no longer exists — search results, blog posts, and a very large amount of model training data all predate the split.

Five experiments, three and a half run

The differentiator is meant to be original measurement rather than assertion. Five experiments are specified; three and a half have been run, and every claim resting on the others stays explicitly labelled as judgement.

Ch. 3 — concurrency20 requests over real sockets: sequential 2.039 s, gather 0.126 s, TaskGroup 0.125 s, capped-at-5 0.421 s. 16× from a one-line change, and the two concurrent forms are indistinguishable.
Ch. 13 — serviceFastAPI under uvicorn, mocked provider. 1→40 concurrent: p50 flat at ~103 ms, throughput 10→349/s. One request in twenty blocking cost 44% of throughput and degraded the nineteen that were innocent.
Ch. 12 — contextTen turns, realistic tool payloads. Projecting tool results cut billed tokens 84%; summarising history cut 35% — and with projection the summariser never fires at all.
Ch. 11 — topologiesStructural half only. Model calls: single agent 2, pipeline/swarm 3, orchestrator and supervisor 5. Three of a supervisor's five calls are routing rather than work. The quality half still needs a provider.

Still outstanding: cache hit rate, tool-selection accuracy, and whether a supervisor actually answers better than a single agent.

The mini-project

The book builds one system across its length, a stage per chapter: Ops Copilot, an internal operations assistant served over FastAPI, backed by Postgres, that can take exactly one irreversible action and only behind a human approval gate.

Every stage's tests pass without a single real model call. A stage that needs a live provider to demonstrate anything has been designed wrong.
